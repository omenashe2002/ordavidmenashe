I am a third-year law student at Fordham Law. I focus on digital assets, DeFi, and AI, and my coursework covers blockchain, smart contracts, and DeFi alongside securities regulation, corporate finance, and securitization.

## Writing

My note, *Registered Agents of the DAO*, starts from the gap between liability and enforcement. Courts treat DAOs as general partnerships, but the doctrine reaches only defendants visible enough to find, so *Samuels v. Lido DAO* caught prominent VCs while the protocol’s architects went unreachable. The note proposes a federal framework: DAOs with a U.S. nexus designate a registered agent and maintain a liquid reserve fund, and exchanges that list noncompliant governance tokens face independent liability. It is forthcoming in the Fordham Journal of Corporate and Financial Law in 2027. I am also Senior Notes Editor of the Journal. The current draft is on this site: [Registered Agents of the DAO](/writing/registered-agents-of-the-dao/).

With Zach Zager and Jake Weintraub of Brooklyn Law School, I wrote *Who Pays When the Agent Pays?*, which won first place in the 2nd Paul Hastings Fintech Crypto Legal Hackathon. It asks who bears the loss when an AI agent is tricked into paying the wrong party, and proposes a framework that channels liability through a bond, an attestation, and an insurance pool. [Read the paper](/writing/who-pays-when-the-agent-pays/).

Each week I write a newsletter, [*If Only I Knew*](/writing/newsletter/), on what is changing in law, AI, and crypto, and what you might actually do about it.
