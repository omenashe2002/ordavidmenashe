This is a sample article. It exists so you can see how writing looks on the site, and it is safe to delete. Everything below is ordinary text typed into a plain file. The site does the formatting.

## A heading breaks up a long piece

Use two pound signs for a main heading and three for a subheading. The title at the top of the page comes from the settings at the top of the file, so the body of the article starts with a main heading, not a title.

### A subheading works the same way

A paragraph is any block of text separated from the next by a blank line. Put a word between asterisks for *italics*, which is the usual way to set off a case name such as *SEC v. W.J. Howey Co.* A link is the words you want to show in square brackets, followed by the address in parentheses, like [this link to the SEC's website](https://www.sec.gov).

## Quotations and lists

Start a line with a greater-than sign to set it off as a quotation:

> A quotation sits in its own indented block, with a thin copper rule beside it. Longer quotations stay readable on a phone.

A numbered list keeps its order, and a bulleted list does not:

1. The first item.
2. The second item.
3. The third item.

- A bullet.
- Another bullet.

## Citations

Legal writing leans on citations, so footnotes are built in.[^first] Type a marker like the one at the end of the previous sentence, then write the note anywhere in the file, usually at the bottom.[^second]

[^first]: A footnote appears at the end of the article, with a link that jumps back to the spot where you were reading.

[^second]: Notes are numbered automatically, in the order the markers appear in the text.
